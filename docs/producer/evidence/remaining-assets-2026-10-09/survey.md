## What remains

The owner is right: Popcorn still prominently uses primitive art. The previous architecture pass preserved it deliberately because it is an interactive construction toy, rather than treating it as completed Fortnite dressing. The largest safe improvement is its green cone/sphere perimeter planting and canopy presentation, followed by coherent native food-production scenery outside the jump and shooting envelope. The blue machines themselves deserve a separate lifecycle-aware art design.

`primitives.json` contains 718 render-enabled primitive components across 371 actor labels: 617 Engine BasicShapes components and 101 VerseEngineAssets components. This is NOT 718 visible defects or a replacement quota. It includes original collision slabs and walls underneath accepted 045/046 skins, educational symbols and objects parked by runtime logic. ## Gameplay ownership and implementation conditions

`popcorn-bindings.json` freshly resolves native savedActor references for all 7 course_decks, 24 course_mechanisms and 53 course_decor. Source `Content/fn_shoreline_island_popbridge_production.verse` validates counts and uses ordered ranges: geometry_changed shows/hides decor and mechanisms (about lines 206-220), committed moves/shows branch/finale decor (222-243), reset_presentation restores transforms and visibility (245-255). The name decor is not evidence of a passive object. Popcorn replacements would need to retain actor bindings, dimensions, support tops, target centers, materials that convey meaning and complete lifecycle behavior. A permanent noncolliding overlay still fails when the original disappears.

Other source checks: Prompt Workshop and legacy Prompt controller move Pix/cores/reactor/gates; event_station moves chute, scanner, speaker and Pix; debug_station teleports marker and goal; signal_station parks and restores examples; nursery_station teleports plants/robot; route_demonstration teleports crate/robot and changes footprint materials. These objects are conditional redesign candidates, not bulk mesh-swap targets.

Candidate bounds and material readback establish asset availability and pivot arithmetic only. Mesh collision, walkable support shape and in-game memory/performance were not assessed. For passive dressing, choose NoCollision when original gameplay collision must remain; visual mass still needs clearance and shot/board visibility review. When replacing a passive bench or planter's physics, match a measured visible footprint deliberately rather than leaving invisible old collision wings. Use native default materials; do not stretch textured art to cover massive structures.

Suggested next scope: a focused Popcorn landscape/canopy and machine-presentation plan, three promenade bench replacements, measured Discovery/Prompt planter upgrades, and a small number of distinct lab service props. Present exact transforms and lifecycle treatment before implementation. User walkthrough remains the runtime acceptance path; this survey made no changes.
