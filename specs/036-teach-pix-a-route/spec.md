# Teach Pix a Route — revision 1

Status: concrete proposal for human review, 2026-10-03. The user's “go for it” selected the Producer direction; it did not approve this subsequently created layout. Feature 035 remains an unimplemented alternative.

Source brief: `docs/producer/hangar-new-gameplay.md`. Optional solo hangar activity for ages 8–10. The player's walked route is the input: demonstrate, watch Pix copy it carrying a crate, encounter a changed bay, then demonstrate a better route. Target first play 2–3 minutes, without a countdown; measure this in playtest.

## Player experience

1. At the home pad, press **SHOW A ROUTE**. “Walk to the loading pad. Your footprints teach Pix.” A broad six-meter practice strip is outlined on the floor. Actual walked XY points appear as footprints; curved, zigzag and indirect safe demonstrations are approximated by recorded points, not replaced by a route ID. Walk into the loading pad to finish recording.
2. Press **TRY MY ROUTE** beside the loading pad. Pix visibly carries a crate along those footprints at 2 m/s. Player can walk alongside or watch; the robot does not require an escort. Actual arrival lights delivery 1.
3. The robot/crate reset to home with a short visible “New delivery” cue. A clearly marked safety closure appears across the middle of the old practice strip, and the two wide bypasses become available. Press **TRY OLD ROUTE** at the loading pad. Pix follows the same demonstrated route and stops safely before the closure. The blocked footprint flashes and the board says “The bay changed. Show Pix a new route.” Delivery 1 stays lit.
4. Walk back to home around either side and press **SHOW NEW ROUTE**. Old footprints clear. Walk any safe route around the closure to the same loading pad; test it. Pix follows the new demonstration, with no autonomous detour. Actual robot-and-crate arrival lights delivery 2 and holds “You showed a route. You tested it. You improved it.” Replay is available; walk-out is always open.

This is a recorded-demonstration simulation, not actual machine learning. The recap adds “This robot copies your example. People can teach AI in other ways too.” No ninth badge, timer, score, weapon task or progression prerequisite.

## Requirements

- R-01 Preserve the user's new Hangar shell, floors, doors, shelves, toolbox, stairs, catwalk and neighboring games. Confine new devices to the measured ground-floor playground; keep the west entrance and 3 m walk-in/out route open.
- R-02 Demonstration records the owner's real grounded XY movement, at most 128 waypoints / 60 m. No predefined-route selection or snapping intermediate points to lane centers. Two different safe first demonstrations must produce visibly different replay traces. Only endpoints may connect to the pad centers within 1 m.
- R-03 Footprints appear during recording and remain for testing. Current playback position and unsafe segment are visibly identifiable without sound. Initially use the broad practice strip; when conditions change, visibly open both bypasses. No invisible obstacle or unexplained restriction.
- R-04 Playback follows stored line segments at 2 m/s, turning in place at corners; it never cuts a smoothed corner or searches for a path. Robot plus carried crate fits a 1.2 m diameter circular ground envelope at every yaw. Validate the whole segment against the declared permitted floor and inflated closure before moving into it. Stop before unsafe space, retain the crate and highlight the rejected segment; do not crash or teleport through it.
- R-05 Delivery 1 requires successful movement readback, correct crate identity and robot plus crate actual position at the loading pad. After the closure, testing the unchanged first route must visibly stop before it. No second-delivery credit until a newly demonstrated safe path actually arrives with the crate. A callback, visual effect or phase flag alone cannot award success.
- R-06 The initial six-meter strip is wide enough for different player-drawn paths and entirely crossed by the closure, guaranteeing the old-route lesson. For revision, both five-meter physical bypasses around the closure are permitted; neither is the hidden preferred answer. Indirect safe routes are accepted without time/score penalties.
- R-07 Invalid recording (jump, out-of-zone point, large transform jump or unsafe segment) stops and marks the last safe footprint, with a specific short reason and SHOW AGAIN. Do not silently bridge the gap. Pauses add no samples. Repeated/backtracked positions are valid. Overlength recording stops gracefully with “Route full. Show a shorter example”; delivery 1 remains. Rerecord clears only the current demonstration; no penalty or timer.
- R-08 One owner controls the shared robot and footprints. Other players cannot record, test, replay or cancel that run. Cancel, departure from the activity envelope, respawn, disconnect or round restart cancels pending playback before resetting all transient state. Replay starts delivery 1 anew; rerecord after the closure preserves delivery 1. Existing loadout, Academy rewards, matchmaking and other activities remain untouched.
- R-09 Physical closure is a non-colliding safety hologram with striped footprint and two visible posts, not a player trap or real-time collision sensor. It blocks the robot through explicit geometry checks. Controls, route marks and closure do not obstruct the walking surface. Explain “Pix won't cross the striped safety area”; crossing it while recording gives an immediate rejection, not a hidden failure at the far pad.
- R-10 Build changed Verse, validate, cook and independently verify the actual final production scene. No gameplay task completes solely on tool success or static geometry. Stop playtest before final handoff.

## Acceptance scenarios

| ID | Given / When / Then |
|---|---|
| AC-01 | Given an uncoached arrival, when the player approaches, then they find Show a Route, understand the footprint instruction, walk normally to Load and find Try My Route. Record confusion and duration. |
| AC-02 | Given fresh runs, when the player demonstrates two visibly different paths within the first strip (one near each edge, with a turn), then stored waypoints and Pix's observed path correspond to each demonstration, not one fixed route. |
| AC-03 | Given a valid first route, when tested, then Pix carries the correct crate, follows its segments, reaches the pad within positional tolerance, deposits the crate and lights only delivery 1. |
| AC-04 | Given delivery 1 and closure shown, when Try Old Route is pressed, then the original route is replayed until its first unsafe segment, Pix stops before the striped area with crate, the affected mark highlights and delivery 1 persists. |
| AC-05 | Given that stop, when a new lower-side route is walked and tested, then actual arrival grants delivery 2. Repeat a fresh run using the upper-side bypass; both work. |
| AC-06 | Given recording, when the player pauses, backtracks or uses an indirect safe path under limits, then it remains valid. Jump, out-of-bounds, oversized displacement, closure crossing and overlength each produce the specified safe stop and retry, without silently shortening the path or losing delivery 1. |
| AC-07 | Given playback near a corner/closure, when the path is tested, then the combined crate/robot footprint stays in allowed space through its four-frame (0.2 s) in-place turns. No more than 128 footprint props are visible; unused pool entries hide and marked positions match saved points within 0.25 m. Falsify a prop endpoint or move the crate away in a controlled QA setup; success must not occur merely because playback ended. Remove fixtures and retest final production. |
| AC-08 | Given any active recording/playback/finale, when Cancel, Replay, departure, respawn, disconnect or round restart occurs, then old movement stops and never later reappears. New run has clean props/footprints; rerecord alone preserves delivery 1. |
| AC-09 | Given a spectator, when they walk, press controls or approach the load pad, then the owner's route and progress do not change. Test real two-player isolation when available without changing matchmaking; otherwise record it unverified. |
| AC-10 | Given normal walking, muted audio and standing/crouched views, when the full activity is played, then footprints, closure, robot/crate, control labels and feedback remain clear; exit is always usable. Ask the player what changed and why Pix needed another demonstration. |
| AC-11 | Given the final production implementation, when build/validation/cook, adjacent activity and spawn/respawn regressions run, then prior behavior remains. Final game state must be not Running; record warnings honestly. |
